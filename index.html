#

about

I'm a sophomore Electrical Engineering student at UCLA (GPA 3.78) with a growing focus on the intersection of embedded control and computer vision. Most of what I build ends up being some flavor of "sensor sees something, microcontroller reacts" — trackers, self-driving carts, environmental monitors. I've interned assembling and testing hardware for aerospace and automotive clients, and outside of that I'm usually debugging a UART line or tuning a PID loop until the jitter finally goes away.

What keeps me hooked on this field is the moment a system that only existed as a schematic and a block of code starts reacting to the real world — a rover finding its target, a feeder recognizing my cat, a sensor catching drift before it becomes a problem. I like projects with a hard deadline attached: most of what I've built has come out of 24-to-48-hour hackathon sprints or a single free weekend, which forces fast decisions over perfect ones and teaches you a lot about what actually matters in a build. I've also spent a few years in more people-facing leadership, organizing volunteers and coordinating community service initiatives, which is a very different kind of problem-solving from embedded systems but scratches a similar itch of getting a lot of moving parts to work toward one outcome. Lately I've been teaching myself the fundamentals of ROS2 and ML architectures on the side, since most of what I want to build next lives at the boundary of controls and autonomy.
